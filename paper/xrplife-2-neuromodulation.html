Neuromodulation as ledger balances: a negative test of the serotonin/PDF behavioural switch on a ledger-resident C. elegans connectome

Julien Cugny

Independent researcher · github.com/LeDenti · 4 November 2026 · preprint, version 1 · companion to "A complete connectome as ledger objects" (same repository, release v1.0.0)

Abstract. The wired connectome of C. elegans does not contain the animal's internal states; hunger, satiety, roaming and dwelling are carried by diffusely released monoamines and neuropeptides that change the gain of many synapses at once. We added this "wireless" connectome (Bentley et al. 2016) to a living ledger-resident worm (161 neurons, 675 chemical synapses, XRPL testnet): each neuron that secretes serotonin or PDF-1 issues its own currency; each receptor is a trust line held by the receiving neuron with the receptor gene in a memo; concentration is the balance; secretion is a payment; reuptake is the balance returned to the emitter. The off-chain agent that propagates activity reads these balances and scales each neuron's output by exp(0.5 (E − I)), where E and I are the fractions of its excitatory and inhibitory modulatory inputs that are active. We posed 994 receptor lines (verified on-ledger), set three anchored moods (baseline, fed with serotonin, hungry with PDF-1) and propagated the same two stimuli in each, 1,026 payments in all, to test the prediction of Flavell et al. (2013) that serotonin promotes dwelling and PDF-1 roaming. The modulators changed total activity (÷1.9 under serotonin, ×1.8 under PDF-1) but not its distribution between forward-locomotion and stop/reversal commands: the roaming index moved by less than 0.015 and in the predicted direction for neither stimulus. The reason is structural: a uniform gain on a neuron scales all of its outputs equally, so the share reaching each command depends on the map alone. A behavioural switch needs receptor effects at the synapse, which neither the dataset nor this model provides. A first trial with one shared currency per modulator also revealed that reciprocally modulating neurons cancel each other on a shared ledger object; one currency per emitter removes the artefact. This is the first measurement in the project that could be wrong, and it was.

1. Introduction

In a companion paper we materialised the wired connectome of C. elegans on the XRP Ledger testnet, neuron by account and synapse by trust line, and ran a complete life cycle of four animals. That construction is true by construction: it reproduces a map. The present paper asks the first question on which the ledger model can fail. Behavioural states of the worm are set by neuromodulators released outside synapses: serotonin from NSM, ADF and HSN signals food and promotes dwelling; the neuropeptide PDF-1 promotes roaming (Flavell et al. 2013). These signals form a second, "wireless" network whose edges are defined by gene expression: an emitter expresses the biosynthetic enzyme, a receiver expresses a cognate receptor (Bentley et al. 2016; for neuropeptides more completely, Ripoll-Sánchez et al. 2023). Time scales matter here: synaptic transmission takes a millisecond and cannot live on a ledger that closes every four seconds, but neuromodulation acts over seconds to hours, the ledger's own pace. If the layered model is right, the modulatory layer is the one the ledger can carry natively. If it is wrong, this is where it should show.

4. Protocol

Three moods, each read from the ledger six seconds after the last secretion and anchored: baseline (no modulator), fed (PDF-1 cleared, serotonin secreted), hungry (serotonin cleared, PDF-1 secreted). In each mood the same two stimuli were propagated for three hops: AWCL+AWCR (food odour) and ALML+ALMR (anterior touch), each starting with activity 1 split between the pair. Readout: the activity delivered to forward-locomotion command neurons F = {AVBL, AVBR, PVCL, PVCR} and to stop/reversal/turn neurons S = {AVAL, AVAR, AVDL, AVDR, AVEL, AVER, RIML, RIMR, RIVL, RIVR, SMDDL, SMDDR, SMDVL, SMDVR}; roaming index = F / (F + S). Prediction (Flavell et al. 2013): fed < baseline < hungry. We fixed in advance a margin of 0.01 below which a difference is reported as no effect.

The modulators act on the amount of activity. From AWC, serotonin divides the activity reaching the commands by 1.9 (its receivers AIA, AIB, AIY, AIZ, RIM, RIB, RIS carry mostly inhibitory receptors) and PDF-1 raises it slightly; from ALM, PDF-1 multiplies it by 1.8 (ALM, AVD, PVC, FLP, OLL, URX all express pdfr-1).

They do not act on its direction. From AWC the roaming index is 0.207 in both modulated moods, 0.0002 apart; from ALM it falls from 0.503 (fed) to 0.492 (hungry), the opposite of the prediction and at the margin. The prediction fed < baseline < hungry is not held for either stimulus.

6. Why the model cannot produce the switch

In this model a modulator changes a neuron's gain, and the gain multiplies every output of that neuron by the same factor. Activity is then distributed by synaptic weight. The fraction of a neuron's output that reaches F rather than S is therefore fixed by the map; modulation changes how much flows, not where. A behavioural switch requires that serotonin changes which synapses transmit: a receptor acting on a specific synapse or a specific target, a change of sign, or a threshold that silences a pathway. Flavell et al. found exactly that: serotonin acts through MOD-1 on specific interneurons to suppress the roaming circuit, and PDF-1 through PDFR-1 on others. Bentley's expression-based map says who can hear a modulator, not what each synapse does when it does. The negative result is therefore informative about the model, not about the worm: a gain layer over a weighted graph is not enough to carry a behavioural state, however faithfully the gains are read from a ledger.

7. What the ledger taught in passing

  • Shared currencies cancel. In a first trial each modulator was a single currency ("PDF1"). The four RME neurons secrete PDF-1 at one another; on the ledger two accounts share one RippleState per currency, so RMED's payment to RMEL and RMEL's payment to RMED netted to zero: 12 of 892 edges were inactive after a successful secretion, measured by re-reading balances. One currency per emitter removes the artefact; it is the same lesson as one currency per neuron for synapses. The first trial is kept in the repository (HUMEUR-essai1.md).
  • Reading too early. Balances read two seconds after the last batch missed 2 of 34 inputs on some neurons; the ledger validated them a moment later. Reading one ledger after the last confirmation fixed it.
  • Who may close a line. An emitter cannot set NoRipple on a line where it is the debtor (tecNO_PERMISSION); the receiver closes its own lines.
  • Cost. 994 receptor lines immobilised 239.8 XRP of reserve, returned when the lines were closed; secretion, clearance and propagation cost 10 drops per transaction. Six propagations took 31 to 117 s each.

8. Limitations

NSM, the main serotonergic neuron for food signalling, is pharyngeal and absent from the nerve-ring dataset: ADF stands in. The receptor map is inferred from gene expression, not from measured transmission. The gain function, the proportional distribution of activity, the three-hop horizon, the all-or-nothing secretion and the roaming index as a readout are all invented; the readout in particular is a crude proxy for a behavioural state that the living animal expresses over minutes. One animal, one stage, two stimuli. None of this simulates physiology; it tests whether a ledger-resident map plus ledger-resident balances suffice to reproduce a known switch under the simplest gain model. They do not.

9. Discussion

The negative result does not weaken the case for the ledger as a carrier of neuromodulatory state: the balances were set, cleared, read and anchored at the ledger's pace, by anyone, with no server to trust, and the experiment can be re-run from the repository against the public endpoint. It weakens the case for a particular model, uniform gain over a weighted graph, and says what the next model must contain: a per-synapse or per-target receptor effect. Two routes are open. The ledger route keeps the map on-chain and moves the receptor effect into the trust line itself: a modulator would change the limit of specific synaptic lines, which is the ledger's native way of saying "this synapse transmits less". The data route replaces Bentley's expression map by measured modulatory effects where they exist (Flavell's MOD-1 targets, the neuropeptidergic map of Ripoll-Sánchez et al. 2023 with its ligand–receptor affinities). Both are a few hundred transactions away. A third use follows directly from the construction: a drug is a rule on the token. A serotonin-reuptake inhibitor is the prohibition of the clearance payment; an antagonist is a frozen receptor line; a dose is an amount. Each such experiment is a sequence of public transactions, which is a modest but real property for a computational pharmacology model: it cannot be adjusted after the result.
